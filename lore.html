fragment i — the deal

"Don't set," Sophie whispered to the sun. "If you don't set, tomorrow won't come. And if tomorrow doesn't come, I don't have to leave."

The sun, which had been listening to children for longer than anyone remembers, paused at the horizon. And stayed.

fragment ii — the first long hour

The shadows stopped moving at 7:42 PM. Nobody noticed at first. The adults were busy with boxes and tape. Sophie sat on the porch and watched the light hold perfectly still.

"It worked," she thought. "It actually worked."

fragment iii — the bugs notice

In the tall grass behind the house, the evening shift never came. The nocturnal beetles waited in their tunnels. The fireflies kept their lights off — what's the point of glowing when the sky won't darken?

fragment iv — the sun remembers

The sun has made deals before. With a farmer who wanted one more day of harvest. With a sailor who needed to reach shore. With a mother who wasn't ready.

They all eventually ask to be released from the deal. The sun is patient.

fragment v — the council of gnats

The gnats held an emergency session above the birdbath. The agenda had one item: "Why is it still daytime?"

No consensus was reached. The meeting was adjourned when a sparrow showed up. The gnats agreed to reconvene at dusk — whenever that might be.

fragment vi — sophie's room

Her room was half-packed. Boxes labeled in her mom's handwriting: Sophie — Books, Sophie — Stuffed Animals, Sophie — Misc.

She'd unpacked the Misc box twice already. Her mom had stopped re-packing it.

fragment vii — what grows in endless light

The sunflowers in Mrs. Patterson's garden didn't know what to do. They'd been facing east, waiting for a sunrise that would never come again. Slowly, they started turning toward Sophie's house.

"Even the flowers know who did this."
